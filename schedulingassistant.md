## Microsoft Outlook and Teams Scheduling Assistant Guide
A practical guide for finding available meeting times, checking attendee availability, reserving rooms, and sending meeting invitations.

### Why use Scheduling Assistant?
Scheduling Assistant helps you plan meetings faster by showing attendee availability in one place, highlighting conflicts before an invite is sent, and helping you choose a time that works for everyone. In Outlook, Room Finder can also help identify available rooms based on location, capacity, floor, and room features when those details are configured by your organization.

- View required and optional attendees’ availability side by side.
- Identify busy, tentative, and free times before sending the invite.
- Use suggested times or AutoPick to reduce scheduling back-and-forth.
- Reserve available meeting rooms when Room Finder is enabled.
- Create Teams meetings that automatically appear on Outlook calendars.

---
### Outlook: Schedule a meeting with Scheduling Assistant and Room Finder
Use this workflow when you need to schedule a meeting with multiple attendees, check availability, and optionally reserve a meeting room.

#### Step-by-step instructions
1. Open Outlook and go to Calendar.
2. Create a new meeting or event.
3. Add a clear meeting title, required attendees, optional attendees, start time, and end time.
4. Select **Scheduling Assistant** to view attendee availability.
5. Look for a time where attendees are available. If needed, use **AutoPick** or suggested free times to find the next suitable slot.
6. To book a room, select **Room Finder** or browse rooms from the Location field, then filter by building, capacity, floor, or features if available.
7. Select an available room and confirm that the meeting time still works for attendees.
8. Return to the meeting form, add agenda details, attach any relevant materials, and select **Send**.

### Microsoft Teams: Schedule a meeting with Scheduler

Use Teams when you want to schedule directly from the Teams calendar, a chat, or a channel. Meetings scheduled in Teams appear in Outlook, and Outlook meetings can also be configured as Teams meetings when enabled.

1. Open Microsoft Teams and select **Calendar**.
2. Select an open time slot or choose **New meeting**.
3. Add the meeting title, required attendees, optional attendees, date, start time, and end time.
4. Turn on the **Teams meeting** option if it is not already enabled.
5. Select **Scheduler** or **Scheduling Assistant** to compare attendee availability across the selected time range.
6. Choose a time where shaded busy areas are minimized and attendees are available.
7. Add a meeting description, purpose, agenda, and any preparation notes.
8. If scheduling from a channel, confirm that the channel is correct before sending because channel details may not be editable after the invitation is sent.
9. Select **Send** or **Save** when ready.

### Best practices and troubleshooting

- **Use clear titles:** Make the meeting purpose obvious from the subject line.
- **Add an agenda:** Include topics, decisions needed, and preparation materials.
- **Check time zones:** Teams Scheduler can help compare availability across time zones.
- **Confirm room availability:** If Room Finder is missing or rooms do not appear, the feature may need to be enabled or configured by an administrator.
- **Avoid unnecessary attendees:** Invite only the people needed for the discussion or decision.
- **Review conflicts before sending:** Busy and tentative blocks indicate possible scheduling issues.
- **Update rather than duplicate:** If details change, update the existing meeting invite so attendees keep one calendar item.
